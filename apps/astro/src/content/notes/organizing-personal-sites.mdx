## どう整理するか

短い学びをこまめに置くには、Simon Willison のように専用の入口があると書き始めやすい。いちど調べたことを育てるには、Martin Fowler や Andy Matuschak のように、疑問や概念ごとにページを持ち、あとから追記したり別のノートにつないだりできるとよい。図や手順を何度も使うなら、Brendan Gregg のようにテーマ名から直接たどれると探しやすい。

最初の一覧は Dan Luu のように簡素でよい。読む順番に意味のあるノートが増えたら、Julia Evans のテーマ別一覧や Joel Spolsky の読書リストが参考になる。Paul Graham の冒頭3本は、主記事の代表作に読者を案内するときに使える。

## junkawasaki.com で採用した方針

- **入口を分ける。** 主記事は既存の `/posts/`、学習ノートは同じサイトの [`/ja/notes/`](/ja/notes/) と [`/en/notes/`](/en/notes/) に置く。ホームには学習ノートへの短いリンクを設ける。
- **ひとつの問いにひとつのURL。** 日英で共通のスラッグを使い、公開日とトピックを一覧に表示する。本文は問いから始め、確認できたことを短く書き、図や比較表、一次資料を必要に応じて添える。
- **同じ問いなら追記する。** 図の修正や参考文献の追加は元のページに入れる。比較の軸や問いが独立したら別のノートにして相互にリンクする。たとえば [DB とファイルシステムの11構造](/ja/notes/storage-structures/) は全体の地図として使い、B+ tree と LSM-tree の書き込み特性を実測するなら別のノートにする。
- **主記事には、まとまった主張を置く。** 複数のノートをもとに自分の考えや検証結果を読者向けに組み立てられたら、主記事として書き、元のノートを資料として参照する。

現在の一覧は日付順で、各行にトピックを添えている。テーマをたどりたい場面が増えたらトピック別の一覧を作る。読む順番が重要なテーマには短い読書リストを添える。既知のノートを一覧から探しにくくなったときは、検索を検討する。

<En>

I want to be able to find a diagram or a comparison again after I have moved on to something else. The main posts have room for developed ideas. A learning note can answer a single question or hold a comparison I expect to revisit. I looked at ten public sites with two questions in mind: **where does each kind of writing go, and how do you find it later?**

## Ten examples

| Writer | How the site is organized |
| --- | --- |
| Paul Graham | Essays share a title index, with three suggested starting points at the top. A new reader gets a short list even as the archive grows. [Essays](https://www.paulgraham.com/articles.html) |
| Simon Willison | The Weblog links to a separate TIL (Today I Learned) site with a topic index, search, and its own Atom feed. [Weblog](https://simonwillison.net/) · [TIL](https://til.simonwillison.net/) |
| Martin Fowler | The Bliki gives short or still-forming ideas a place to appear as they arise and accumulate into a collection of linked pieces. [What Is a Bliki](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/WhatIsaBliki.html) |
| Andy Matuschak | The main site highlights selected Working Notes. In the notebook, he publishes notes made for his own use and readers can follow links between them. [Main site](https://andymatuschak.org/) · [Working Notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/About_these_notes) |
| Julia Evans | The blog puts the ten latest posts above the full category index. Wizard Zines provides a separate entry point for illustrated booklets. [Blog](https://jvns.ca/) · [Wizard Zines](https://wizardzines.com/) |
| Joel Spolsky | The home page combines recent posts with a Top 10 and reading lists for audiences such as new developers and tech leads. [Joel on Software](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/) |
| John Gruber | The Linked List gives external links and short commentary their own monthly archive. Those items also appear in the home-page stream. [Home](https://daringfireball.net/) · [Linked List](https://daringfireball.net/linked/) |
| Dan Luu | Technical analysis, measurements, and miscellaneous writing share a simple dated title list. [Index](https://danluu.com/) |
| Nikita Prokopov (tonsky) | Blog, Work, and Talks have their own entrances. The blog lists articles by year. [tonsky.me](https://tonsky.me/) |
| Brendan Gregg | A chronological blog sits alongside subject pages such as Flame Graphs and Linux Perf, where readers can return to instructions and examples. [Home](https://www.brendangregg.com/) · [CPU Flame Graphs](https://www.brendangregg.com/FlameGraphs/cpuflamegraphs.html) |

## What to borrow

A dedicated entrance like Simon Willison’s makes it easy to keep publishing small discoveries. Giving each question or concept its own page, as Martin Fowler and Andy Matuschak do, lets it grow through revisions and links. When a diagram or procedure is likely to be used repeatedly, Brendan Gregg’s subject pages show the value of a link readers can revisit by topic.

A simple title list in the style of Dan Luu is enough at the beginning. Once readers have enough material to benefit from a path through a topic, Julia Evans’s category listings and Joel Spolsky’s reading lists offer two ways to organize that path. Paul Graham’s short list at the top of Essays is a useful model for introducing the main writing.

## The approach adopted on junkawasaki.com

- **Give each kind of writing an entrance.** Main articles live under the existing `/posts/` routes. Learning notes have their own [Japanese](/ja/notes/) and [English](/en/notes/) indexes on the same site, with a small link from the home page.
- **Give each question a stable URL.** Both languages share a slug. The index shows a date and a topic for each note. Start with the question, write down what you found, and add diagrams, comparisons, and primary sources as needed.
- **Update the page when the question stays the same.** Fix a diagram or add a reference in place. When a new question can stand on its own, give it a page and link the two. The [11 storage structures note](/en/notes/storage-structures/) serves as an overview. A measured comparison of B+ tree and LSM-tree writes would warrant its own note.
- **Use a main post for a developed argument.** When several notes support an argument or a set of results worth presenting to a reader, write that post and cite the notes it draws on.

The current index is chronological and shows each note’s topic. A topic index becomes useful once readers regularly want to browse a subject. Add a short reading list when the order matters, and consider search when the index makes it hard to find a known note.
